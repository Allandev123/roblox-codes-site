A RIVALS loadout is the four weapons you take into every duel. You start with a free set, and every other weapon costs Keys, so your first few buys matter.

This page covers how the slots work, the cheap weapons worth buying, the Wildcat SMG after its buff and which weapons suit which maps.

Prices, stats and map info are from Update 23 (September 18, 2026), the patch that buffed the Wildcat.

## How loadouts work

Every loadout has four slots: **Primary**, **Secondary**, **Melee** and **Utility**. Everyone starts with the same free weapon in each one.

| Slot | Free starter weapon |
|---|---|
| Primary | Assault Rifle |
| Secondary | Handgun |
| Melee | Fists |
| Utility | Grenade |

Everything else has to be unlocked. Weapons come in three price tiers: Standard weapons cost up to 75 Keys, Prime weapons 200 to 550, and Contraband weapons 600 to 700. A few can't be bought with Keys at all, like the gamemode-exclusive ones and the Jump Pad (it uses Jump Shards).

### Loadouts in ranked

In ranked, each team bans one map and two weapons. The starter weapons can only be banned once every player in the duel owns more than four weapons in each slot. So while you own four or fewer in a slot, your starter there is safe.

You can also only switch weapons twice per duel. That's why it helps to own a backup in each slot, in case a weapon you bought gets banned.

## Is the free loadout any good?

Mostly, yes. The fan wiki lists "great for beginners" as one of the Assault Rifle's pros, and one Update 22 tier list puts it in S tier, with the Handgun and Grenade in A tier.

So you don't need to rush to replace it. Your first Keys go further on weapons that do a different job, like winning close-range fights.

## Cheap weapons worth your Keys

These are the cheapest weapons in each slot, with prices checked in late September 2026.

| Slot | Cheapest options (Keys) |
|---|---|
| Primary | Bow 15, Shotgun 20, Burst Rifle 25, RPG 25, Crossbow 35, Wildcat 35, Gunblade 45, Sniper 75 |
| Secondary | Spray 5, Flare Gun 10, Shorty 20, Uzi 20, Revolver 25, Daggers 30 |
| Melee | Chainsaw 15, Knife 20, Scythe 40, Katana 45, Riot Shield 45, Battle Axe 50 |
| Utility | Smoke Grenade 10, Flashbang 15, Molotov 25, War Horn 30, Satchel 35, Freeze Ray 45 |

Four of these have a very clear job:

- **Shotgun (20 Keys).** A close-range primary that fires 10 pellets per shot. The closer you are, the more damage it does, so it shines in tight spaces and around corners.
- **Uzi (20 Keys).** A fast-firing secondary for finishing off weak opponents up close. Its damage drops hard with distance, and it chews through ammo.
- **Revolver (25 Keys).** A secondary with really high damage per shot. Its Fanning ability fires every bullet left in the chamber at once, but the spread is huge, so save it for close range.
- **Katana (45 Keys).** A melee that can deflect bullets back at the enemy for a short window. It can't deflect splash damage or projectiles like the RPG, Bow or Crossbow.

That same tier list names the Uzi and Shotgun as the first cheap upgrades to buy. It also puts the Katana and Scythe in A tier.

### Where prices jump

After the Daggers (30), the next cheapest secondaries are the Slingshot at 300 Keys, the Exogun at 350 and the Energy Pistols at 400. Melee jumps from the Battle Axe (50) straight to the Trowel (200) and the Maul (550).

The Medkit might look like an easy first buy, but it's a Prime item that costs 300 Keys. The Subspace Tripmine isn't cheap either at 250.

### Getting the Keys

You can earn every weapon on this page with free Keys. They come from daily tasks, Bonus Tasks like Win 12 Duels, weapon and map contracts, codes and the Daily Login Rewards in the Shop. Since Update 22, some Bonus Tasks can only be done three times a day.

My [Keys guide](/guides/rivals-how-to-get-keys/) covers each one, and the [RIVALS codes page](/rivals-codes/) lists the codes that work right now.

## The Wildcat after its buff

The Wildcat is a Standard primary SMG that came out in Update 22 in early September 2026. It costs 35 Keys in the weapon shop, and once you buy it, it's yours for good.

Two things make it different from every other gun:

1. **Real recoil.** It's the first weapon in RIVALS with realistic recoil. Your camera kicks upward while you fire, so you have to pull your aim down to stay on target. It's fully automatic, but short bursts are easier to control than holding the trigger.
2. **The jungle reload.** It uses two magazines taped together. The first reload is very fast, and the next one takes about twice as long. Getting caught on that slow second reload in the middle of a fight hurts.

### What Update 23 changed

The developers said the Wildcat didn't perform as well as expected on release, so Update 23 buffed it.

| Stat | Update 22 | Update 23 |
|---|---|---|
| Damage | 9 | 10 |
| Crit damage | 12 | 13 |
| Magazine | 24 | 30 |
| Reserve ammo | 96 | 120 |
| Damage dropoff range | 30 to 90 studs | 40 to 100 studs |

Its damage now starts falling at 40 studs and bottoms out at 2.2 per hit at 100 studs.

### What it's good at

The Wildcat does full damage inside about 40 studs, so it's a close-range gun. It fits small maps with lots of cover and tight corridors. At long range it barely scratches people, and the Assault Rifle or Sniper are better picks out there.

Its Eliminations and Playtime contracts each give 1 Key at the first tier (5 kills and 5 minutes of use), so you get 2 of your 35 Keys back quickly. The later contract rewards are cosmetics like wraps, a finisher and a charm.

## Which weapons fit which maps

One loadout guide sums it up with a simple rule. On large maps, run a Sniper plus a pistol. On medium maps, the Assault Rifle plus a pistol. On small maps, an SMG or Shotgun plus a pistol.

### Close-range maps

**Backrooms** is dark, and it's hard to spot opponents from far away. Close-range picks like the Shotgun, Uzi and Wildcat make more sense there than a Sniper.

**Playground** is large and mirrored, but its crates, bouncy tires and wooden walls give it a maze-like layout. Opponents are easy to lose track of, so a close-range secondary is worth bringing even if your primary is a rifle.

### Large maps

**Crossroads** is a large map based on the old Roblox game Classic: Crossroads. The rule points to the Assault Rifle or a Sniper (75 Keys) here, and it's where the Wildcat's dropoff hurts most.

The Big maps (Big Arena, Big Backrooms, Big Crossroads, Big Graveyard, Big Onyx, Big Splash and Big Station) are larger versions of regular maps, made for bigger modes like 4v4 and 5v5. More space usually means more fights at range.

### Maps with their own twist

- **Arena** is a simple mirrored map with no barrels or breakable windows. The fan wiki calls it a straightforward beginner map, so it's a good place to learn a new weapon.
- **Onyx** is a complicated map surrounded by lava, and touching the lava hurts.
- **Construction** is mirrored and full of gaps you can fall through.
- **Station** is a train station with breakable windows.
- **Splash** is an inflatable arena floating on water. Step outside the ring around it and you're eliminated.
